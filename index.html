Why is the atmosphere trapping pollution?

The Planetary Boundary Layer (PBL) is the lower part of the atmosphere directly influenced by the Earth's surface. A lower PBL reduces the volume available for pollution to mix and disperse.

✅ Normal / Higher PBL — Better Dispersion

When the PBL is high, polluted air can mix vertically over a larger volume, diluting PM2.5 concentrations near the surface.

⚠ Low PBL + Inversion — Pollution Accumulates

When the PBL is low — especially with a temperature inversion — PM2.5 is trapped in a shallow layer near the ground, causing rapid concentration increases.

🔍 Why is the risk high?

AeroShield is flagging a high episode risk because several atmospheric conditions are occurring simultaneously. Here's what each factor means in plain language:

🌫️
Low Planetary Boundary Layer (PBL)

The Planetary Boundary Layer is the lowest part of the atmosphere that is mixed by surface heating and turbulence. When it is low, there is less vertical space for polluted air to mix. Think of it like a low ceiling in a room — smoke from a candle fills the room faster in a low-ceiling room than a high one. A PBL of ~520 m is much lower than the normal 1,200–2,000 m range.

💨
Weak Wind Speed (1.3 m/s)

Wind normally carries polluted air away from the city and replaces it with cleaner air. At only 1.3 m/s, the wind is very weak — polluted air is not being carried away quickly. The air mass over the region becomes stagnant, allowing PM2.5 to keep accumulating from ongoing emission sources (vehicles, industry, agriculture burning).

🌡️
Temperature Inversion Detected

Normally, air temperature decreases with altitude, which allows warm surface air to rise and mix with cooler air above. A temperature inversion is when a layer of warm air sits above cooler surface air — this acts like a lid on the atmosphere. It actively suppresses vertical mixing, preventing pollutants from dispersing upward. This is one of the most critical drivers of severe pollution episodes.

📈
Rising PM2.5 Trend

PM2.5 concentrations have been rising over the past several hours, even before the atmospheric conditions have reached their most unfavorable state. This means pollution is already building up before the expected episode. As trapping conditions worsen, there is a higher "pre-existing" concentration to accumulate further.

🔗
Why These Factors Combine to Create High Risk

Each of these factors individually would cause some concern. Together, they create a "perfect storm" for pollution accumulation: a shallow boundary layer limits the mixing volume, weak winds remove the horizontal escape route, and a temperature inversion blocks the vertical escape route. With PM2.5 already rising, continued emissions from traffic and industry have nowhere to go.

Summary: AeroShield is warning because the atmosphere is creating conditions where pollutants emitted near the surface cannot disperse — they can only accumulate. This is a prototype demonstration of how atmospheric understanding can provide earlier warning than simple AQI threshold alerts.
